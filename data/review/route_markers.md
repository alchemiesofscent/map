# Periplus route markers

> Wrap each place name you want to "visit" with single asterisks (`*Name*`)
> or double asterisks (`**Name**`) — both work. The order of markers in a
> section is the order the map will focus on each place. Multi-word names
> work (`*Ptolemais of the Hunts*`). The same place can be marked again in
> a later section. After editing, run `python3 scripts/import_route_markers.py`.
>
> Each section block keeps its source-of-truth translation in plain prose.
> The HTML comment under each header lists every place the upstream extractor
> tagged for that section — useful as a hint when you choose what to mark.

About three thousand stades after Ptolemais of the Hunts lies the lawful trading station Adulis, set in a deep south-facing bay. The island called Oreine lies before it, where ships now anchor because of attacks from the mainland; formerly they anchored deeper in the bay at Diodoros island. On the mainland opposite Oreine, twenty stades from the sea, is the village of Adulis. From it a three-day road leads to inland Koloe, the first ivory market; five more days lead to the metropolis of the Aksumites, where all ivory from beyond the Nile is brought through Kyeneion and then onward to Adulis. Elephants and rhinoceroses mostly range in the upper country, though rarely near coastal Adulis. Offshore islands called Alalaiou have tortoise shell carried to the emporion by the Fish-Eaters.

Into these places go unfulled Barbarian cloths made in Egypt, Arsinoite robes, imitation colored cloaks, linens, double-fringed cloths, many kinds of glass-stone and other murrhine from Diospolis, brass used for ornament and cut as money, copper vessels for cooking and for making women's bracelets and anklets, iron for spears against elephants and other beasts and for wars, axes, adzes, knives, large round copper drinking cups, a little denarius coinage for resident foreigners, a little Laodicean and Italian wine, and a little oil. From these places are exported ivory, tortoise shell, and rhinoceros product. Most goods from Egypt are brought to this emporion from January to September, and the timely departure from Egypt is around September.

Farther east the Arabian gulf stretches on and narrows especially at Avalites. After about four thousand stades, sailing east along the same mainland, there are other Barbarian emporia called the far-side markets, with anchorages and roadsteads suitable by season. The first is Avalites, where the crossing from Arabia to the far side is narrowest. At this small trading station rafts and boats come together. Into it go mixed glass-stone, juice of Diospolitan sour grapes, mixed Barbarian cloths, grain, wine, and a little tin. From it are exported aromatics, a little ivory, tortoise shell, and a very small amount of myrrh superior to the rest; sometimes the local barbarians carry these by rafts across to Okelis and Mouza.

After Avalites is another trading station, better than it, called Malao, about eight hundred stades by sea. Its harbor is an open roadstead sheltered by a cape rising to the east, and its inhabitants are more peaceful. Immediately after Leuke Kome the Arabian country extends for a long distance along the Red Sea. Muza is a trading station without a harbor but with good roadsteads and anchorages because of the sandy ground around it. Into it go purple cloth, Arabian garments, saffron, cyperus, linen, cloaks, blankets, girdles, moderate perfume, money, wine, and some grain; the local country also produces wheat and more wine. From Muza are exported local choice myrrh, stacte myrrh, and lybdos, together with the previously mentioned cargoes from the Adulis side. The suitable sailing to it is around September, though earlier is possible.

After Eudaimon Arabia there follows a long connected shore with bays for more than two thousand stades, inhabited by nomads and fish-eaters. Beyond the Calai islands is another coastal place, Canê, in the kingdom of Eleazus and the frankincense-bearing country; opposite it lie two desert islands, Bird Island and Dome Island. The gulf at Barygaza is narrow and hard for ships coming from the sea to enter; they tend to fall either right or left. On the right at the gulf mouth lies the rough, spotted strip called Herone near the village of Kammoni; on the left opposite it is the cape before Astakapra, called Papike, a difficult anchorage because of the current and the rough rocky bottom that cuts anchors.
