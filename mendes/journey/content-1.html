    How to read this

    What a journey is

    Each journey steps through the places one ancient source names as an origin for a single ingredient, in the order that source names them. The stops are not a voyage and not a supply chain: no line joins them, because the sources attest origins, not travel between them.

    Evidence classes

    • Ancient-attested claim. A Greek, Latin, or Egyptian source states this origin.
    • Inference or modern correction. The recipe is silent about provenance, or later evidence corrects the ancient claim.
    • Theological provenance. Divine-origin language, deliberately not pinned as literal geography.

    Route caution

    Every sea, land, and Nile corridor on the companion map is a conventional reconstruction—not a route attested for any single ingredient. This viewer draws no corridors at all.

    Ingredients without a journey

    Fifteen of the twenty-four materials have one attested place or none. They open as a single card rather than a journey, because the sources give no sequence to walk. Source silence stays visible.
